# MedDefense Attack Surface Map

An attack surface is every point where an attacker can interact with MedDefense. A security gap is a weakness on that surface.

## Section 1: External Surface

| Entry Point                       | Asset Behind It                                              | Existing Protection                                               | Documented Gap                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Patient portal**                | `web-srv-01`, patient accounts and portal data               | Located in the DMZ behind the FortiGate 100F; HTTPS is enabled    | TLS 1.0 is still enabled. The portal admin panel has no MFA under **M-05**, and activity is not centrally monitored under **M-04**.                                                                                                  |
| **Public website**                | `web-srv-01` on ports 80 and 443                             | FortiGate perimeter firewall and DMZ placement                    | Website logs are stored locally, no SIEM or automated alerting exists under **M-04**, and no web application firewall is documented.                                                                                                 |
| **Remote-access VPN**             | FortiGate 100F, Active Directory and the internal network    | FortiGate authentication, password policy and local firewall logs | **M-05: No MFA** allows stolen passwords to be used. **M-04** means unusual VPN activity may not be detected. Once connected, **M-01** allows broad internal access.                                                                 |
| **Westside site-to-site VPN**     | `ws-srv-01`, Westside workstations and Central systems       | IPSec tunnel to the Central FortiGate                             | **M-06:** Westside uses a consumer Netgear router with no managed firewall. VPN rules permit broad access to the Central server network.                                                                                             |
| **Corporate HQ site-to-site VPN** | HQ workstations and Central servers                          | Separate landlord VLAN and encrypted VPN tunnel                   | MedDefense cannot assess Greenfield’s network security. The VPN allows all services toward the server subnet, increasing the effect of an HQ compromise.                                                                             |
| **Microsoft 365**                 | Email, SharePoint, OneDrive and possibly Entra ID identities | Microsoft cloud protections and the MedDefense password policy    | **M-05:** MFA is not enabled. No DLP is deployed, and O365 data is not included in MedDefense backups.                                                                                                                               |
| **Public DNS**                    | Domain records for the website, portal, VPN and O365 email   | DNS enables users to locate legitimate MedDefense services        | The DNS provider, registrar controls, MFA and domain-monitoring process are not documented. This increases typosquatting and DNS-account takeover risk.                                                                              |
| **Billing web service**           | `billing-srv-01`, billing and claims processing              | FortiGate is the main perimeter protection                        | The server runs Ubuntu 18.04 and Apache 2.4.29 with a known remote-code-execution weakness. The previous crypto-miner shows that attackers have already found and exploited this service. Server endpoint protection is also absent. |

The internal asset list confirms that `web-srv-01` hosts both the public website and patient portal, while the network design uses the FortiGate as MedDefense’s main internet boundary. The assessment also confirms no MFA, no centralized monitoring and weak VPN trust paths.

## Section 2: Internal Surface

### Flat Network Risk

**M-01: Network Segmentation** is the main internal attack-surface problem. The scan confirmed that a workstation at Corporate HQ could reach the Central servers, workstations, medical devices and Westside systems without internal restrictions. A compromise of one device can therefore expose nearly the entire organization.

| Asset or Interface                   | Internal Exposure                                                                               | Why It Matters in a Flat Network                                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **`billing-srv-01`**                 | SSH 22, HTTP 80 and MySQL 3306                                                                  | MySQL is accessible from the entire network. Any compromised workstation could attempt database access. The server also runs an old OS and previously contained a crypto-miner. |
| **`ehr-db-01`**                      | SSH 22 and PostgreSQL 5432                                                                      | PostgreSQL is reachable network-wide instead of only from `ehr-srv-01`. This creates a direct route to restricted patient records.                                              |
| **`ehr-srv-01`**                     | SSH 22, HTTPS 443 and application port 8080                                                     | It is the main EHR application server. SSH is protected with keys, but the application remains reachable from across the flat network.                                          |
| **Active Directory controllers**     | DNS 53, Kerberos 88, LDAP 389/636, SMB 445 and RPC 135                                          | A compromised internal device can directly target the domain controllers, steal credentials and gain control of other systems.                                                  |
| **`NAS-01` management**              | Ports 5000, 5001 and SSH 22                                                                     | Its management interface is reachable across the network. An attacker could delete or encrypt backups, supporting **M-02: Backup Isolation**.                                   |
| **FortiGate administration**         | Administrative web or SSH interface; exact management IP and port were not recorded in the scan | If reachable from general workstations, stolen administrator credentials could allow firewall and VPN changes. No MFA or central alerting protects administrative access.       |
| **PACS server**                      | SMB 445 and medical imaging ports 4242 and 11112                                                | A compromised workstation could access imaging services. Radiology also uses the shared `raduser` account under **M-07**, reducing accountability.                              |
| **Medical monitors**                 | HTTP 80, HTTPS 443 and port 2575                                                                | Their management pages are reachable throughout the network. Default credentials and outdated firmware could allow an attacker to affect clinical monitoring.                   |
| **Infusion pumps**                   | HTTP 80 and HTTPS 443                                                                           | The pumps have known security concerns and are not isolated. Compromise could affect medication delivery, supporting **M-03: Medical IoT Exposure**.                            |
| **Nurse call systems**               | HTTP 80 and SIP 5060                                                                            | A workstation compromise could reach systems used for patient requests and clinical communication.                                                                              |
| **Badge readers**                    | HTTP 80 and HTTPS 443                                                                           | Network access may allow attempts to interfere with physical-access systems.                                                                                                    |
| **Reception and admin workstations** | RDP 3389, SMB 445 and RPC ports                                                                 | RDP has no network-level restriction. Attackers can use these systems for credential theft and lateral movement.                                                                |
| **MRI workstation `WS-RAD-01`**      | Windows XP with ports 135, 139 and 445                                                          | Windows XP is unsupported and cannot receive modern security updates. The flat network connects this legacy system to critical servers.                                         |
| **`print-srv-01`**                   | Windows Server 2012 R2, ports 135, 139, 445 and 9100                                            | **M-08** identifies this as end-of-life. Although it is low value, it could provide a weak internal foothold.                                                                   |
| **Undocumented devices**             | `10.10.2.99`: 22, 8888, 9090; `10.10.10.200`: 22, 80, 3000                                      | IT does not know who owns or maintains them. They could contain weak credentials, unpatched software or unauthorized services.                                                  |

The scan directly confirms network-wide database access, unrestricted NAS management, exposed RDP and medical-device web interfaces. The main related gaps are **M-01**, **M-02**, **M-03**, **M-04**, **M-07** and **M-08**.

## Section 3: Human Surface

| Role                                 | What They Can Access                                                                     | Why They Are Targetable                                                                                                                                | Training or Control Gap                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Clinical staff**                   | EHR records, PACS, patient information, medication systems and clinical workstations     | They work under time pressure and are trained to help patients quickly. Phishing, vishing and fake IT requests may cause them to bypass normal checks. | Training completion is only 71% at Central and 58% at Westside. There is no healthcare-specific or role-based training. Shared PACS credentials also exist under **M-07**. |
| **Reception staff**                  | Patient registration data, appointment details, EHR functions and reception workstations | They are the first contact for visitors and callers. Their helpfulness can be exploited through impersonation, pretexting or tailgating.               | Reception computers expose RDP. Physical security training is generic, and security coverage is limited outside normal hours.                                              |
| **IT staff**                         | Domain administration, VPN, firewalls, servers, backups and endpoint tools               | Their accounts offer high privileges. A small 12-person team, ticket pressure and fatigue may lead to shortcuts or successful targeted phishing.       | No role-specific administrator training, no privileged-access management, no MFA under **M-05**, and no formal change-management process.                                  |
| **Executives and finance**           | Strategic documents, legal information, email and payment authority                      | Their authority makes them valuable BEC and impersonation targets. Attackers can request transfers or sensitive documents using urgency and secrecy.   | HQ training completion is high at 94%, but no executive-specific BEC training or transfer verification training is documented.                                             |
| **External contractors and vendors** | EHR maintenance, medical-device servicing, building networks and support systems         | Their access and security practices are partly outside MedDefense’s control. A stolen vendor account may appear trusted.                               | Vendor access is not consistently time-limited, protected by MFA or centrally monitored. MedDefense also lacks full visibility into Greenfield’s infrastructure.           |
| **Former staff and contractors**     | Potential retained VPN, application or local accounts                                    | Accounts may remain active after employment ends and can be used by the former user or anyone who obtains the password.                                | Offboarding is manual, and there is no automated link between HR records and account disabling. **M-04** also limits detection of off-hours access.                        |

Training records show low completion among clinical staff, no phishing simulations and no role-specific training for clinical teams, IT staff or executives. Physical protection is also limited to one weekday guard at Central, with no patrol coverage and weak coverage at Westside and HQ.

## Surface Assessment Summary

The **internal surface currently represents the greatest risk** because **M-01: Network Segmentation** turns every successful external or human attack into a possible organization-wide compromise. An attacker who steals one employee password, compromises one workstation or enters through a vendor connection can directly reach the EHR database, domain controllers, backups and medical devices. **M-04: Absence of Monitoring** allows this movement to remain hidden, while **M-03** adds possible patient-safety consequences. The external and human surfaces are likely entry points, but the flat internal network is what allows one entry point to become a major hospital incident.
