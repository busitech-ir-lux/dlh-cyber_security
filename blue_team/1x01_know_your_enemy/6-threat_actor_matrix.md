Healthcare intelligence shows that 78% of reported breaches were hacking or IT incidents, while network servers were involved in 43% and electronic medical records in 16%. The same intelligence identifies ransomware as MedDefense’s leading threat, negligent insiders as highly likely and opportunistic attacks as already occurring.

MedDefense’s most important weaknesses are connected. One compromised account or workstation can reach clinical systems because the network is flat, while the lack of MFA and centralized monitoring makes entry and detection easier. The backup NAS is also reachable from production systems, increasing ransomware impact.

# Top 3 Priority Ranking

## 1. Ransomware Groups — Organized Crime

Ransomware groups represent the greatest overall threat because both **likelihood and impact are Critical**. MedDefense matches the preferred victim profile: a mid-size hospital with valuable patient data, limited security staffing and systems that cannot tolerate downtime. The likely attack path is already visible: exploit the FortiGate or steal a VPN password, cross the flat network, compromise Active Directory, steal EHR data, encrypt production systems and destroy accessible backups. A similar regional hospital suffered ambulance diversions, 11 days of downtime and millions of dollars in losses after this same sequence.

## 2. Insider — Negligent

Negligent insiders rank second because they are highly likely and can create serious patient-data or operational incidents without intending harm. MedDefense’s environment contains several examples: shared PACS credentials, incomplete training, personal storage devices, weak password handling and manual offboarding. A single mistake can expose patient records directly or create an entry point for an external attacker. Because **M-04** prevents effective monitoring, MedDefense may not discover the activity until data has already been disclosed.

## 3. Unskilled or Opportunistic Attacker

Opportunistic attackers rank third because MedDefense has already experienced this threat through the crypto-miner on `billing-srv-01`. These actors do not need to select MedDefense personally; automated scanners continuously search for old software, exposed services and default credentials. Their individual capability is low, but **M-01**, **M-03**, **M-04** and **M-06** allow a simple initial compromise to become a larger incident. The impact may range from resource theft to EHR disruption or patient-safety risk if the attacker reaches connected medical devices.
