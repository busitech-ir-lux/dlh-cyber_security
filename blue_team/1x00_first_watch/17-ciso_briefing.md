# MedDefense Board Briefing

## Opening Statement

MedDefense can provide patient care today, but its security controls are too weak for the level of risk it carries. A single successful attack could disrupt clinical operations across the hospital, Westside Clinic and Corporate Headquarters.

## Critical Finding

The most dangerous weakness is the flat internal network: once an attacker enters through one device, they may be able to reach servers, staff computers and medical equipment. In a real incident, this could shut down the electronic health record, delay imaging, interrupt medication systems and force clinicians back to paper for days.

## Three Priority Actions

**1. Segment the network and isolate medical devices.** Separate clinical systems, servers, staff computers and medical equipment so one infected device cannot expose the whole organization. Estimated cost: **$45,000**. Timeline: **more than one month**.

**2. Require multi-factor authentication and strengthen privileged access.** This adds a second proof of identity so a stolen password alone cannot unlock remote or administrator access. Estimated cost: **$17,000**. Timeline: **within one month**.

**3. Create offsite, protected backups and a tested incident-response plan.** This gives MedDefense a clean recovery path and clear actions during ransomware or a major outage. Estimated cost: **$26,400**. Timeline: **within one month**.

## The Business Case

The proposed **$120,000 security budget** is an investment in continuity, patient safety and legal protection. A comparable regional hospital ransomware attack caused 11 days of downtime, $5 million in recovery and lost revenue, cancelled procedures and regulatory investigation.

## Closing

If the Board takes no action, MedDefense remains exposed to a preventable hospital-wide incident that could harm patients, stop revenue and cost many times more than the proposed investment.
