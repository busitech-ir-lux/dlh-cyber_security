## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy defines how MedDefense systems, devices, networks and information may be used. Its purpose is to protect patient care, patient privacy, financial information and hospital operations from misuse, malware, data loss and unauthorized access.

This policy applies to:

* employees
* physicians
* contractors
* students
* temporary staff
* volunteers
* vendors with access to MedDefense systems

It covers:

* computers and clinical workstations
* medical devices
* servers and databases
* email and internet access
* Wi-Fi and internal networks
* VPN and remote access
* cloud services
* mobile phones and tablets
* removable media
* patient, employee and financial information

All users must follow this policy while using MedDefense-owned systems or accessing MedDefense information.

## 2. Acceptable Use of Systems

MedDefense systems are primarily provided for authorized healthcare, administrative, research and business activities.

Users may:

* access information required for their assigned work
* use approved applications and cloud services
* communicate with patients, employees and approved partners
* use limited personal internet access when it does not affect work, security or patient care
* connect approved MedDefense devices to the network
* report suspicious emails, system activity or possible security incidents
* access systems remotely through approved VPN services

Users must follow least privilege and access only the information required for their role.

During a clinical emergency, patient safety takes priority. Emergency access must still be recorded and reviewed after the event.

## 3. Prohibited Activities

Users must not:

* share usernames, passwords, badges, tokens or MFA approvals
* use another employee’s account
* use shared accounts unless formally approved for a technical or clinical reason
* bypass security controls, including antivirus, EDR, MFA, firewalls or web filtering
* install unapproved software, browser extensions or cloud applications
* connect unauthorized devices to the MedDefense network
* create private Wi-Fi networks or use personal routers
* upload MedDefense data to personal email, storage or file-sharing accounts
* access patient records without a valid clinical or business reason
* copy large amounts of patient or financial data without approval
* attempt to scan, test or attack MedDefense systems without written authorization
* disable logging or delete security records
* use MedDefense systems for illegal, abusive or offensive activity
* connect unsupported or unpatched equipment without an approved exception

These rules address risks previously identified at MedDefense, including shared PACS accounts, shadow IT, unapproved network devices, malware infection and unauthorized access to patient information.

## 4. Personal Devices and Removable Media

### 4.1 USB Drives and Removable Media

Personal USB drives, external hard drives and other removable media must not be connected to MedDefense systems.

Only MedDefense-approved encrypted removable media may be used when:

* there is a valid business or clinical need
* the department manager approves the use
* the device is scanned for malware
* sensitive data is encrypted
* the data is removed when no longer required

Users must not copy patient, financial or credential information to removable media unless specifically authorized.

This rule addresses the identified risk of malware and data exposure through unrestricted USB use.

### 4.2 Personal Phones and Tablets

Personal phones may be used only for approved applications and services. Users must not:

* photograph patient screens, records or clinical documents
* store patient information in personal applications
* send patient data through personal messaging platforms
* connect personal devices to restricted clinical or server networks

Personal devices must use the approved guest or employee wireless network.

### 4.3 Personal Laptops

Personal laptops must not connect directly to MedDefense’s internal clinical or administrative network. Remote work must use an approved MedDefense device and the official VPN.

Exceptions require written approval from the IT Director and Deputy CISO.

## 5. Password and Authentication Requirements

Each user must have an individual account. Shared accounts are not allowed unless formally approved and technically necessary.

Users must:

* use long and unique passwords or passphrases
* never reuse MedDefense passwords on personal websites
* protect passwords from other employees, managers and vendors
* use the approved password manager when provided
* report suspected password exposure immediately
* complete MFA when required
* never approve an unexpected MFA request

MFA is mandatory for:

* VPN access
* administrator accounts
* remote access
* critical systems where technically supported

Users must not attempt to avoid MFA or ask another person to approve an authentication request.

IT must disable accounts promptly when employment or access requirements end.

## 6. Data Handling

MedDefense information must be handled according to its classification.

### Public

Information approved for public release, such as public website content and published job advertisements.

### Internal

Information intended for MedDefense employees, such as internal procedures and general meeting notes.

### Confidential

Sensitive business or personal information, including employee records, contracts and internal financial information.

### Restricted

Highly sensitive information, including:

* patient and EHR records
* medical images
* billing and payment data
* passwords and authentication secrets
* encryption keys
* administrator credentials

Users must:

* access confidential and restricted data only when required
* verify recipients before sending sensitive information
* use approved encrypted communication methods
* store data only in approved systems
* lock screens when leaving a workstation
* avoid discussing patient information in public areas
* securely dispose of printed documents
* immediately report accidental disclosure or incorrect delivery

Restricted data must not be stored on personal devices, personal cloud services or unapproved applications.

## 7. Email, Internet and Phishing

Users must be careful when opening links, messages and attachments.

Users must:

* check unexpected requests for payments, passwords or patient information
* report suspicious messages through the approved reporting process
* verify unusual requests using a second communication method
* avoid opening unknown attachments
* never provide passwords or MFA codes by email or telephone

Users must not forward suspicious emails to colleagues. They should report them to the security team.

## 8. Monitoring and Privacy

MedDefense may monitor activity on its systems to protect patients, systems and information.

Monitoring may include:

* login activity
* system and application logs
* internet and DNS activity
* email security events
* file transfers
* USB connections
* endpoint security alerts
* VPN activity
* access to patient records

Users should not expect personal privacy when using MedDefense systems, except where required by law.

Monitoring information will be accessed only by authorized personnel and used for security, compliance, patient-safety and operational purposes.

## 9. Incident Reporting

Users must immediately report:

* suspected phishing
* malware alerts
* lost or stolen devices
* unauthorized access
* accidental data disclosure
* unexpected MFA requests
* suspicious system behavior
* unknown devices connected to the network
* policy violations that may affect security

## 10. Enforcement

Violations may result in:

* additional training
* removal of system access
* formal disciplinary action
* termination of employment or contract
* legal or regulatory reporting
* civil or criminal action where appropriate

Enforcement will consider the seriousness of the violation, intent, patient-care circumstances and previous incidents.

MedDefense may temporarily block a device, account or network connection when necessary to protect hospital operations.

## 11. Exceptions

Exceptions must be:

* required for a valid clinical or business reason
* documented in writing
* risk-assessed
* approved by the IT Director and Deputy CISO
* limited to a defined period
* protected by compensating controls

An exception does not permanently remove the requirement.
