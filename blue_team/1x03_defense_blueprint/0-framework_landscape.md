## Part 1 — Three-Framework Summary

### NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0

NIST CSF 2.0 is published by the **National Institute of Standards and Technology**. It helps organizations understand, manage and communicate cybersecurity risk. It is organized into six functions: **Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond and Recover**, with categories and subcategories under them. It can be used by organizations of any size or sector, including private hospitals.

### CIS Controls v8

CIS Controls v8 is published by the **Center for Internet Security**. It provides practical and prioritized security actions that organizations can implement. It contains **18 Controls**, 153 Safeguards and three Implementation Groups: **IG1, IG2 and IG3**. IG1 is designed as essential cyber hygiene and is a suitable starting point for organizations with limited resources.

### ISO/IEC 27001

ISO/IEC 27001 is published jointly by **ISO and IEC**. It defines the requirements for creating and improving an Information Security Management System, or ISMS. It is structured around management clauses, from organizational context to continual improvement, together with security controls in Annex A. Organizations use it to manage security formally and to demonstrate their security program to auditors, regulators and customers.

## Part 2 — Relationship Map

The three frameworks can work together. **NIST CSF explains what security outcomes the organization should achieve. CIS Controls explains the practical actions needed to achieve them. ISO/IEC 27001 provides the management, documentation and review process needed to prove that security is being managed properly.** MedDefense can therefore use NIST for strategy, CIS for technical implementation and ISO 27001 for governance and audit evidence.

## Part 3 — MedDefense Framework Selection

MedDefense should use **NIST CSF 2.0 as its main strategic framework** because it is flexible, risk-based and suitable for a hospital that is starting without a formal framework. It should use **CIS Controls IG1** to choose the first practical security actions, such as asset inventory, access control, backups and vulnerability management. MedDefense should also gradually align its policies and evidence with **ISO/IEC 27001**, but immediate certification may require more staff, time and money. This combination gives the Board a clear strategy, gives the technical team practical actions and helps demonstrate security governance to regulators.
