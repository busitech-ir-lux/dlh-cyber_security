# 9. The CFO Challenge

## Objection 1: “We have never been breached. Why spend $120,000 now?”

**Acknowledgment:** It is reasonable to question a major investment when MedDefense has not confirmed a large patient-data breach.  
**Counter-Evidence:** However, the billing-server crypto-miner operated for at least two weeks without detection, proving that attackers can enter the environment and remain hidden.  
**Business Framing:** The proposed $120,000 program is much smaller than the estimated annual exposure of the EHR breach and VPN compromise risks, which each exceed $2.8 million.  
**Recommendation:** Fund the program now because security investment is intended to prevent a predictable loss, not wait until the loss has already happened.

## Objection 2: “Your ALE numbers are estimates, not facts.”

**Acknowledgment:** The ALE figures are estimates, and changes in incident frequency, impact or downtime will change the results.  
**Counter-Evidence:** Even if the estimated benefits were reduced by 50%, controls such as MFA and network segmentation would still provide risk reduction far above their combined cost.  
**Business Framing:** ALE is not a guarantee; it is a transparent planning method that allows the Board to review and challenge every assumption instead of making decisions based on intuition.  
**Recommendation:** Approve the controls but require MedDefense to review the estimates every six months using real incidents, downtime, audit findings and control-performance data.

## Objection 3: “Insurance is cheaper than controls.”

**Acknowledgment:** Cyber insurance is useful because it can transfer part of the financial impact of a serious incident.  
**Counter-Evidence:** The policy covers only up to $1 million, while the estimated impact of a complete EHR breach is approximately $9.075 million, leaving a major uninsured loss.  
**Business Framing:** Insurance may not fully cover service downtime, patient-safety consequences, reputation damage, future premium increases or losses caused by failing to maintain required security controls.  
**Recommendation:** Keep the insurance policy, but treat it as a supporting risk-transfer measure rather than a replacement for prevention, detection and recovery controls.

## Objection 4: “This should be IT’s regular budget, not a special ask.”

**Acknowledgment:** Security and IT activities overlap, and some security costs should normally be included in the IT operating budget.  
**Counter-Evidence:** The current IT budget has not produced MFA, centralized monitoring, network segmentation or isolated backups, showing that these improvements require protected funding and clear ownership.  
**Business Framing:** A separate security allocation gives the Board visibility into how much is being spent to reduce regulatory, operational and financial risk.  
**Recommendation:** Approve the first-year security allocation separately, then move recurring infrastructure costs into IT’s normal budget after responsibilities and service levels are formally defined.

## Objection 5: “Can we start with $60,000 and see if it works?”

**Acknowledgment:** A phased investment is reasonable for a new security program if the first phase produces measurable results.  
**Counter-Evidence:** A $56,000 first phase could fund MFA for $12,000, network segmentation for $35,000 and the Westside firewall for $9,000.  
**Business Framing:** These controls address credential compromise, lateral movement and the weakest remote site, while leaving $4,000 as contingency.  
**Recommendation:** Accept the phased approach only if the Board commits to reviewing defined results within six months and considers funding monitoring, EDR and offsite backups in the next phase.

# Closing Statement

The proposed program costs **$120,000**, while MedDefense’s largest individual risks have estimated annual losses ranging from **$300,000 to more than $3 million**. These figures should not all be added together because some attack paths overlap, but they still show that the cost of inaction is much higher than the control budget. The recommended controls reduce the likelihood of a breach, limit attacker movement, improve detection and protect recovery data. The investment therefore supports cost avoidance, regulatory readiness, service continuity and patient trust.
