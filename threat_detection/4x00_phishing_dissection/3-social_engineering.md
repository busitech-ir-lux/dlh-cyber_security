3. Social Engineering Analysis

Email 2 — Portal verification lure

Psychological lever: Urgency, fear, authority, and impersonation.

Pretext: The message claims that a MedDefense security policy update requires Diane Marsh to re-verify her staff portal access.

Requested action: Click the verification link and verify the account.

Targeting level: TARGETED

Content red flags:

Claims to come from “MedDefense IT Security.”

Uses a strict 24-hour deadline.

Threatens loss of access to the scheduling system, EHR gateway, and shift swap requests.

Uses “ACTION REQUIRED” and a prominent “VERIFY MY ACCESS NOW” button.

References Diane Marsh by name.

Includes an internal-looking ticket number to appear legitimate.

Attacker knowledge required:

Diane Marsh’s name.

Her MedDefense email address.

Knowledge that she is a staff member.

Awareness of MedDefense systems such as the staff portal, scheduling system, EHR gateway, and shift swap process.

Conclusion: This is a targeted credential-harvesting style lure built around a believable internal IT/security pretext. The attacker uses urgency and fear of losing access to push Diane into clicking quickly.

Email 3 — Microsoft account warning lure

Psychological lever: Fear, urgency, authority, and impersonation.

Pretext: The email claims that Microsoft detected an unusual sign-in attempt on Rafael Mendez’s Microsoft 365 account.

Requested action: Click the link and verify the account.

Targeting level: SEMI-TARGETED

Content red flags:

Uses the display name “Microsoft Account Protection.”

Claims a suspicious login from Lagos, Nigeria.

States that the account may have been compromised.

Threatens account lockout within 48 hours.

Uses a “Verify account” button.

Includes Rafael’s MedDefense email address in the warning.

Uses Microsoft branding and a Microsoft corporate address to look official.

Attacker knowledge required:

Rafael Mendez’s name.

His MedDefense email address.

Knowledge that MedDefense staff may use Microsoft 365.

Conclusion: The lure is designed to create fear of account compromise and pressure the recipient to act quickly. It is more personalized than a generic phishing message, but it relies mostly on common Microsoft 365 account-security themes.

Email 5 — Invoice lure

Psychological lever: Financial pressure, urgency, authority, and impersonation.

Pretext: The email claims that MedDefense owes USD 24,716.38 for medical supplies delivered on April 9, 2026 and that payment is due within seven days.

Requested action: Open the attached invoice, visit the payment portal, or log in to retrieve the invoice.

Targeting level: TARGETED

Content red flags:

Sent directly to Angela Rivera in Accounts Payable.

Uses a realistic invoice number: INV-2026-04891.

Gives a specific amount and due date.

Threatens suspension of future deliveries and a 2% late fee.

Includes both a payment link and a login portal.

Contains a PDF attachment named like a normal invoice.

Uses a supplier/billing pretext that matches the recipient’s role.

Attacker knowledge required:

Angela Rivera’s MedDefense email address.

Knowledge that she works in Accounts Payable.

Awareness that MedDefense would plausibly purchase medical supplies.

Understanding of common invoice and supplier-payment workflows.

Conclusion: This is a targeted business-email-style lure aimed at Accounts Payable. It uses realistic financial details and consequences for late payment to create pressure and make the request fit Angela’s normal work responsibilities.

Email 7 — Benefits enrollment lure

Psychological lever: Urgency, fear, scarcity, authority, and impersonation.

Pretext: The message claims that Linda Patterson has not completed her 2026 benefits re-enrollment and that open enrollment closes the next day.

Requested action: Click the enrollment link and complete or verify benefits enrollment.

Targeting level: TARGETED

Content red flags:

Claims to come from “MedDefense HR Benefits.”

Addresses Linda by name.

Uses a hard deadline: midnight the next day.

Threatens loss of current coverage.

Claims the employee will be moved to a basic plan until the next enrollment period.

Uses “FINAL NOTICE” and “COMPLETE ENROLLMENT” to increase pressure.

Tells already-enrolled employees to verify anyway, increasing the chance of a click.

Attacker knowledge required:

Linda Patterson’s name.

Her MedDefense email address.

Knowledge that she is a MedDefense employee.

Awareness of employee benefits and open-enrollment processes.

Conclusion: This is a targeted HR-themed lure. It combines fear of losing benefits with a short deadline and MedDefense impersonation to push Linda toward the fake enrollment portal.
