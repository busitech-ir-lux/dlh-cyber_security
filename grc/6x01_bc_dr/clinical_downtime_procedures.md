# Clinical Downtime Procedures

**Version:** 1.0 | **Prepared:** 2026-11-10 | **Status:** Proposed; clinical approval pending

Print the two references below on separate sheets after clinical review. Source:
`nursing_station_locations.md`, `paper_forms_inventory.md`, and
`clinical_escalation_contacts.md`. Unit-specific locations take precedence over
the inventory's general descriptions. No drill or staff acknowledgment is claimed.

## Epic EHR Downtime Procedures

1. **Start safe paper documentation immediately when records are unavailable.**
   Tell the charge nurse. The Administrator on Call, extension **7000**, authorizes
   hospital-wide downtime. The Nursing Supervisor, **7100**, coordinates units;
   the Clinical Applications Lead, **7300**, confirms record availability with
   the incident lead at **7200**. Do not wait for a hospital-wide announcement
   before reporting a care concern or recording essential care on paper.

2. Find your unit's downtime forms. In ICU, use the red “Downtime Forms” binder
   at the charge nurse desk; medication administration record packets are in
   the locked lower drawer below the charge nurse workstation. Allergy cards
   are beside the medication room door. In ED, the binder is in cabinet 3,
   top shelf, at the charge station; medication forms are in the Pyxis room
   wall file. On Medical-Surgical, the binder is in the central station's blue
   rack; medication forms are above the barcode scanner docking station.

3. Confirm the patient's identity using the approved identifiers and wristband.
   Label every assessment, order, and medication sheet with the patient details,
   date, time, and your name. Keep one organized patient packet. Protect paper
   from public view and do not leave it unattended. Never fill a missing allergy
   or medication history by guessing; ask the responsible clinician and pharmacist
   to resolve the uncertainty.

4. Use paper assessment sheets for observations and paper medication administration
   records for medication documentation. Obtain physician order sheets and
   reconciliation cover sheets from the unit clerk desk; Medical-Surgical order
   sheets are in its right cabinet. **Document each administration at the time
   of care and check existing paper entries to prevent duplicate doses.** Use
   the verbal-order read-back form in the binder when applicable: repeat the
   instruction, obtain confirmation, and record the names and times.

5. Escalate unresolved problems from primary nurse to charge nurse, Nursing
   Supervisor, attending physician, then Administrator on Call. For medication
   concerns, involve the pharmacist on duty at **7400** before the attending
   physician. Use the unit's emergency escalation process immediately for a
   deteriorating patient; a records outage must not delay emergency assessment.

6. **Continue paper procedures until the authorized return-to-service notice.**
   The charge nurse and clerk collect packets with reconciliation cover sheets.
   Assigned staff enter outstanding orders and completed care with original
   event times and identify entries made after downtime. Reconcile medication
   records, pending tests, and duplicate orders with the responsible clinician.
   Confirm completion, retain original sheets under the records procedure, and
   report missing or conflicting entries before closing the unit's downtime log.

## Laboratory Information System Downtime Procedures

1. **Report missing laboratory orders or results immediately; start the paper
   process rather than waiting for the screen to recover.** Tell the charge
   nurse and contact laboratory accessioning at **7500**. The Administrator on
   Call, **7000**, authorizes hospital-wide downtime. The Nursing Supervisor,
   **7100**, coordinates units, and the Clinical Applications Lead, **7300**,
   confirms laboratory availability with the incident lead at **7200**.

2. Find the laboratory's yellow “LIS Downtime” binder at the accessioning desk.
   STAT requisitions are in tray 1 and routine requisitions in tray 2. The
   manual specimen tracking log is at the accessioning desk, and the specimen
   rejection log is in tray 3. The critical-value call log is on the chemistry
   bench clipboard. In ED, laboratory requisitions are also on the specimen
   collection cart, second drawer. Ask the charge nurse for the unit binder
   when working outside the laboratory.

3. Write the patient full name, medical record number, date of birth, collection
   date and time, collector initials, requested test, and ordering provider on
   the requisition and required specimen label. Match the patient, label, and
   order before collection and handoff using the approved identification process.
   Record priority and a working unit callback contact. **Do not guess missing
   identifiers or relabel a mismatched specimen from memory.** Accessioning
   records receipt and disposition in the tracking log; record rejection and
   contact the ordering team under the approved specimen procedure.

4. For a STAT order problem, escalate from ordering clinician to accessioning
   at **7500**, chemistry lead at **7510**, then laboratory medical director at
   **7520**. Confirm that the receiving person has the order and specimen details.
   Keep forms in controlled staff areas and use approved phone contacts; do not
   send patient information through personal messages or leave it in public view.

5. **A critical result requires a direct call and read-back.** The laboratory
   calls the ordering unit. The receiver repeats the patient identity and result;
   the laboratory confirms accuracy and records receiver name, time, and read-back.
   If there is no answer within **5 minutes**, escalate to the charge nurse;
   at **10 minutes**, escalate to the Nursing Supervisor. Record every attempt
   and escalation. The receiving clinical team follows its approved urgent-care
   process without waiting for electronic result posting.

6. Continue paper tracking until the authorized restoration notice. Laboratory
   and unit staff reconcile requisitions, received specimens, pending tests,
   completed results, and critical-value calls. Enter results with original
   collection and reporting times, check patient and specimen matching, and
   identify already-actioned results to prevent duplicate orders or treatment.
   A named reviewer records completion and resolves missing results. Retain the
   original forms; report unresolved items before ending the downtime process.
