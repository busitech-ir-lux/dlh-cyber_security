# Annual Security Awareness Program Plan: MedDefense

**Sponsor:** Dr. Patricia Morales, CISO  
**Operator:** IT Security Awareness Lead  
**Status:** Proposed first-year plan; prepared 2026-11-10  
**Period:** February–January following approval; year labels confirmed at sign-off

## 1. Executive Summary

The March 2026 incident delivered 41 phishing messages; 23 recipients clicked and six reported, with a 74-minute mean reporting delay. Credential exposure and Cobalt Strike activity were confirmed; ePHI exfiltration was not confirmed. Healthcare-specific messages exploited expected workflows and fear of lost Epic access. IR activation, containment, forensics, resets and executive communications incurred cost, but the materials provide no dollar estimate. The board must request an incident-cost ledger rather than accept a fabricated loss figure.

The program combines four initial simulation waves over 90 days, immediate five-minute instruction, short role guides, independent-verification practice, safe phone scenarios and private coaching. Measures capture clicking, reporting and verification under realistic conditions, rather than relying on annual course completion. Clinical Operations controls timing; HR protects individual results; CISO authorizes launches. New clinical content and simulations pause during accreditation and flu pressure. No actual deployment or effectiveness is claimed in this plan.

At 12 months, targets are ≤25% overall/clinical clicking, ≥50% reporting, ≤15-minute mean reporting delay, <8% repeat clicking and ≥95% training completion within 72 hours. At 36 months, the supplied targets are ≤12% overall/clinical clicking, ≥70% reporting, ≤10-minute delay, <4% repeat clicking and ≥98% completion. These are commitments for monitoring, not guaranteed causal effects or quantified savings. The $84,000 source estimate plus explicitly modeled workforce time needs board review, with quarterly renewal decisions informed by comparable behavioral results and operational burden.

### Requested Categories Not Separately Priced in the Materials

External benchmarking: use provided course ranges and optional public reports initially; proposed incremental fee **$0**, with no paid engagement assumed. If independent comparable healthcare benchmarking is required, obtain a quote and request a separate line before commitment; the $84,000 contains no identified fee for it.

Program management overhead: the dashboard line supports reporting, not a fully evidenced annual staffing allocation. Proposed incremental model: 0.1 FTE × 2,080 hours × $50 loaded/hour = **$10,400**. HR/Finance must validate staffing and any overlap with the $8,000 dashboard and content costs before addition. Omission leaves approvals, exclusions and data retention without assigned capacity.

Training delivery opportunity cost: proposed planning allowance is four five-minute modules **per employee at most** in the initial campaign plus ten minutes of guide/refresher time annually. At the 1,610 estimate: 1,610 × 30/60 = **805 staff-hours**; at assumed $40 loaded/hour = **$32,200**. This is a budgeting allowance, not expected clicks, a training requirement for every person or a hard maximum for future coaching. Actual cost uses completed minutes × role-specific loaded rates, plus approved tabletop/coaching time. Reconcile roster and clinical backfill needs before approval.

Provisional economic envelope: **$84,000 + $10,400 + $32,200 = $126,600**, excluding unquoted benchmarking, incremental backfill and additional coaching/exercises outside the allowance. Workforce opportunity cost is not necessarily cash expenditure; overhead may be absorbed by existing staffing. Do not present $126,600 as a sourced quote or a complete final appropriation. Finance confirms cash versus absorbed cost and removes overlap. Without protected delivery time, completion and coaching targets are unlikely to be operationally credible.

Use observed incident response cost and attack volume for later scenario analysis, with uncertainty. Do not multiply the percentage-point click reduction by the previous incident's cost and call it savings.

## 4. Governance and Review

| Responsibility | Accountable role and workflow |
| --- | --- |
| Program owner/operator | Awareness Lead schedules, checks gates/exclusions, controls individual clicks and training, manages retention |
| Strategy/content approval | CISO approves launch/high-risk templates; HR privacy review; Clinical Operations/business owner timing and realism; Communications readability |
| Phone/insider safeguards | Legal reviews recorded calls and escalation; HR/Legal approve manager guide and anonymous channel before use |
| Metrics review | SOC Manager validates reports, benign-mail adjudication and workload; Lead validates delivery/people counts; CISO approves quarterly aggregate report |
| Escalation authority | HR confirms coaching trigger; Legal joins conduct/privacy/regulatory cases; separate SOC incident response for real compromise |
| Managers | Support availability and report role changes; receive aggregate data only subject to HR rule |

Lead/SOC review operations weekly and close each wave after the seven-day observation window. HR reviews privacy/support issues monthly. CISO and board receive quarterly counts, trends, intervals, exclusions and decisions; six-month review uses the brief template. Manager confidence survey repeats annually using the same scale/respondent frame.

Proposed lifecycle: review every template before reuse and at least quarterly; retire any with exposed content, misleading cues, distress concerns, obsolete workflows or unexplained difficulty drift. Reassess segment risks quarterly using IAM changes and incident reporting. A real phishing incident during a wave triggers immediate send pause, SOC incident routing, separation of real/simulation labels and preservation of relevant evidence; do not continue to “test” responders. HR may halt for welfare and Clinical Operations for safety; CISO approves restart only after relevant owners clear the issue. No campaign deadline overrides a pause.

## 5. Board Success Criteria

Targets at 12/36 months below are supplied CSV values; 18-month commitments come from the benchmark file and campaign plan. **24-month values are proposed** interim targets. Baseline: 56% overall clicks, 15% reports, 74-minute reporting delay, 67% clinical clicks; other metrics unknown/not measured.

| Outcome | 12 months | 24 months (proposed) | 36 months |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Overall click rate | ≤25% | ≤12% | ≤12% |
| Clinical click rate | ≤25% | ≤12% | ≤12% |
| Report rate | ≥50% | ≥67% | ≥70% |
| Mean time to report | ≤15 min | ≤10 min | ≤10 min |
| Repeat clicker rate | <8% | <4% | <4% |
| Training completed within 72 h | ≥95% | ≥97% | ≥98% |
| Finance BEC verification | ≥80% | ≥90% | ≥95% |
| Vishing verification full pass | ≥75% | ≥85% | ≥90% |
| Manager escalation confidence | ≥25% relative improvement | ≥40% relative improvement | ≥50% relative improvement |
| False-positive reports | Within approved SOC capacity | Stable workload within capacity | Stable with validated triage automation |

Confidence improvement means (current mean − baseline mean)/baseline mean ×100, not percentage points. A 1–5 scale has a ceiling: if baseline makes a 50% increase impossible, CISO/HR must approve and disclose a revised attainable target; do not silently cap or fabricate improvement. Program success requires targets met on validated comparable data, no unresolved privacy/patient-care harms and quarterly board reporting. Unknown baselines and inadequate samples remain explicitly unassessed.
