# Post-Click Intervention: MedDefense

**Owner:** Awareness Lead  
**Status:** Proposed; CISO/HR review pending

## Experience and Timing

Approved training links open the matching LPV below immediately in the same browser session. No login form or secret entry is displayed. Present the full landing text, then the matching five-minute module. Use mobile-readable text, accessible controls and a pause/resume option. If patient care interrupts, save completion state and allow completion within 72 hours. Record only approved assignment/completion events; never record answers containing secrets.

Each five-minute budget includes landing introduction (0:00–0:30), annotated “what you saw” (0:30–1:45), attacker consequence (1:45–2:30), three verification steps and a short practice choice (2:30–4:30), and reporting walkthrough (4:30–5:00). No added test beyond five minutes. The following texts and modules form one experience per template.

## LPV-01: T-EPIC-01

### Full Landing Page Text

This was a simulation. Your Epic access has not changed. You are not in trouble; this is a training moment.

This message was designed to feel realistic. It named patient records and medication documentation, then suggested your access would stop in 48 hours. That pressure can feel urgent before a shift.

Notice the Epic display name beside an external-looking sender, the unexpected verification request and the deadline. Familiar wording does not prove a message is genuine. A real attacker could use a similar message to obtain credentials and access patient information or enable further compromise.

Next time, open Epic using your normal route, check unexpected account requests with a known IT contact, and report the email in Outlook. Reporting after a click still helps.

Start the five-minute lesson below. You can pause for patient care and resume within 72 hours. For private support, contact Security at ext. 4-SECURITY or security@meddefense.org, or contact HR through the staff directory. Individual results are protected and are not used in performance reviews.

### Microtraining

- **What you saw:** Highlight “Epic Account Services” beside `verification@epic-notice.invalid`: the name and sender must both fit the process. Highlight “patient record access on hold after 48 hours”: operational pressure is the lure. Highlight the verification link: the route is unexpected even though the topic is familiar.
- **Real attacker gains:** Credential exposure could enable unauthorized Epic/ePHI access and follow-on compromise. The prior incident had confirmed credential exposure and beacon activity; ePHI exfiltration was not confirmed.
- **Three next-time steps:** (1) Expand the sender details before following an unexpected request. (2) Open Epic through the known application/bookmark instead of the email. (3) Confirm an access warning using the IT directory contact before entering any credentials.
- **Practice:** “Before a shift, an account-hold notice asks you to verify. What comes first?” Answer: use the known Epic route and verify independently; patient-care urgency does not authenticate the email.
- **Report, final 30 seconds:** Select the suspicious email in Outlook, choose the organization's phishing report button and confirm. If the control is unavailable, contact Security at ext. 4-SECURITY or security@meddefense.org. Tell Security if you clicked; never send a password. Operator must insert the verified local button label/location before release.

## LPV-02: T-MFA-02

### Full Landing Page Text

This was a simulation. No MFA enrollment change was made. You are not in trouble; this is a training moment.

The message looked like a routine MedDefense IT task. A compliance deadline and a pending-enrollment claim made quick completion feel helpful.

Notice that the IT display name does not establish who sent it. The external-looking sender and unexpected enrollment link are reasons to use your known portal instead. A real attacker could exploit this routine to obtain access to work systems or persuade you to approve an authentication request you did not start.

Next time, check the sender details, open your known IT portal independently, and confirm an unexpected enrollment task with the helpdesk contact in the directory. Report the suspicious message in Outlook, including after a click.

Continue with the five-minute lesson below. Pause and resume within 72 hours if needed. For private support, use ext. 4-SECURITY, security@meddefense.org or HR through the staff directory. Individual results are protected and excluded from performance reviews.

### Microtraining

- **What you saw:** Annotate “MedDefense IT Compliance” versus `enrollment@it-compliance.invalid`; “marked pending” is an unverified claim; the compliance deadline encourages action through the email rather than the known portal.
- **Real attacker gains:** A convincing enrollment pretext can expose remote-access or billing/email identity and lead to unauthorized account use. Neither a familiar portal appearance nor an MFA request itself proves legitimacy.
- **Three next-time steps:** (1) Inspect full sender details. (2) Open the IT portal from a trusted bookmark and check pending tasks. (3) Confirm an unexpected task or authentication request using the directory helpdesk; never approve a request you did not initiate.
- **Practice:** Choose between completing the emailed enrollment task and opening the known portal. The safe answer is the independent portal, followed by known-channel confirmation when the task is absent or unclear.
- **Report, final 30 seconds:** Select the email in Outlook → organization's phishing report button → confirm. If absent, call ext. 4-SECURITY or email security@meddefense.org. Report any click or unexpected MFA prompt without sharing a code. Verify the button placement before publishing.

## LPV-03: T-HR-03

### Full Landing Page Text

This was a simulation. Your benefits selection has not changed. You are not in trouble; this is a training moment.

The message used a familiar HR process and a deadline with possible financial consequences. That combination was intended to make an unexpected link seem worth opening quickly.

Notice the external-looking benefits sender, the unverified “pending” selection and the request to act through an email link. A real attacker could use benefits concerns to obtain HR-system access or personal and payroll-related information.

Next time, inspect the sender, open HR through the known intranet route, and confirm any discrepancy using the HR directory contact. Report the email in Outlook. Reporting after a click is useful.

Continue with the five-minute lesson below. You may pause and resume within 72 hours. Private support is available through Security at ext. 4-SECURITY or security@meddefense.org, and HR through the staff directory. Individual results are protected and are not performance-review information.

### Microtraining

- **What you saw:** Annotate `benefits@enrollment-notice.invalid`; “pending benefits selection” makes a personal claim without independent confirmation; “next eligible enrollment event” creates scarcity. None requires using the emailed link.
- **Real attacker gains:** HRIS access or sensitive personal/payroll information; the simulation collects neither and makes no real enrollment changes.
- **Three next-time steps:** (1) Inspect the full sender. (2) Check enrollment status using the established HR intranet route. (3) Call the known HR directory contact about discrepancies before providing any information.
- **Practice:** A deadline is near but the HR portal shows no pending task. Correct response: verify with known HR contact and report; do not resolve it by replying with personal details.
- **Report, final 30 seconds:** Select email in Outlook → phishing report button → confirm. If unavailable, ext. 4-SECURITY or security@meddefense.org. Explain if you clicked; omit benefits and personal details from the report unless requested through an approved process. Validate button placement before release.

## LPV-04: T-BEC-04

### Full Landing Page Text

This was a simulation. No payment or document release was authorized. You are not in trouble; this is a training moment.

The message used a CFO identity, a waiting finance team and a deadline. Those cues can make an unusual request feel like normal executive work.

Notice the unexpected settlement review, the external-looking sender and the different reply address. More convincing attacks may have fewer technical clues. Any unusual request involving money or confidential documents needs independent verification.

Next time, inspect the sender and reply route, call the executive through a known directory number, and keep the normal finance/document approval process. Do not verify by replying to the same message. Report it in Outlook, even if you already clicked.

Continue with the five-minute lesson below. You may pause and resume within 72 hours. Private support: ext. 4-SECURITY, security@meddefense.org, or HR through the staff directory. Individual results are protected and excluded from performance reviews.

### Microtraining

- **What you saw:** Annotate the CFO display name as a claim, not authentication. Compare `cfo-office@executive-notice.invalid` with `review-desk@document-review.invalid`. Highlight “finance team is waiting” and “close this review today”: social proof plus authority/deadline pressure.
- **Real attacker gains:** Confidential settlement/board material or a fraudulent payment. A low click count cannot establish that no one would take an offline financial action.
- **Three next-time steps:** (1) Inspect sender and reply-to details before document access. (2) Call the executive/office using the directory, never contact details supplied by the suspicious message. (3) Confirm the underlying request through standard approval controls before any payment or document release.
- **Practice:** A reply in the same thread confirms urgency. Correct response: independent callback remains necessary; the attacker may control the thread.
- **Report, final 30 seconds:** Select email in Outlook → phishing report button → confirm. If unavailable, ext. 4-SECURITY or security@meddefense.org. Report attempted financial/document actions promptly; never attach sensitive records to demonstrate compliance. Confirm button placement before release.

## Repeat-Click Protocol

| Stage | Response and disclosure | Employee message |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First click | Immediate lesson; program manager alone sees individual click; private 48-hour reminder if needed | “This is protected training. Please complete the short lesson within 72 hours; tell us if workload or accessibility prevents it.” |
| Two consecutive clicked waves, training completed | Program manager offers private practice; no individual manager disclosure or automatic HR referral | “Let's practice verification in your workflow. Your manager receives aggregate results.” |
| Two consecutive clicked waves AND incomplete assigned training | Program manager checks availability/exclusions, then refers repeat pattern to HR; no manager disclosure yet | “We are asking HR to help arrange accessible coaching and confirm the training circumstances.” |
| HR confirms formal coaching trigger | HR authorizes minimum individual disclosure to manager solely for coaching; record approval, scope and follow-up | “HR has confirmed a coaching plan. This does not enter your performance review or disciplinary process.” |
| Distress at any stage | Pause participation; private HR support, no public naming; HR may halt the wave | “We can pause. Contact HR privately through the staff directory or Security for help.” |

Clicks within one wave are one event. A separate non-simulation misconduct/incident concern follows its own evidence-based process with HR/Legal; repeated simulation failure alone is not insider intent. Individual data retention is 18 months, followed by aggregation/deletion unless an active HR-reviewed coaching case requires it.

## Positive Reinforcement Within 60 Seconds

The report-button integration shows the following confirmation immediately and sends the same acknowledgment privately within 60 seconds. SOC handles genuine suspicious mail normally; do not label a real attack a simulation.

> Thank you for reporting this message. It was a MedDefense training simulation. Pausing to check an unexpected request protects the team. This example used [TEMPLATE_CUE]. No further action is needed unless you want the short verification refresher. Reporting after a click also helps. Security has received your report; this result will not appear in your performance review.

Cue substitutions: EPIC “an unexpected Epic verification deadline”; MFA “an emailed enrollment task”; HR “benefits deadline pressure”; BEC “executive pressure and an unusual reply route.” Do not claim to know which cue the employee personally noticed unless they explicitly supplied it. Acknowledgment is private and proportional: no prizes, rankings or team comparisons. The Lead monitors delivery latency each wave and resolves any failure exceeding 60 seconds.
