# Task 0: Know Your Adversary and Your Audience

## What the briefing establishes

The supplied task contains no quiz questions. These are study notes, not submitted quiz answers. The kickoff describes an Epic-themed message arriving before a nurse's shift and being opened on a phone. The key pressure is maintaining access to patient records under time pressure, not lack of intelligence or care.

The briefing gives 56% MedDefense clicking, 32% healthcare average, 38% without active simulations, and 8% clicking/61% reporting after 18 months of quarterly simulations. These are **course scenario figures** without underlying study methods. They illustrate a design hypothesis; they do not prove causation or justify promising an 8% result. The local planning target is ≤12% clicking and ≥65% reporting at 18 months.

## Decisions to understand before building

| Concept | Meaning at MedDefense | Design consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Authority and urgency | “Epic access will stop” appears relevant to patient care | Teach known-channel verification even when the request feels urgent |
| Familiarity | MFA and compliance messages resemble routine tasks | Open the known portal independently |
| Scarcity | Benefits deadlines create personal financial pressure | Verify through HR; avoid employment or termination threats |
| Social proof and authority | A purported CFO request says others are waiting | Independently verify money and document requests |
| Cognitive load | Shift handoff competes with message scrutiny | Use brief mobile-readable guidance and approved safe windows |
| Spaced reinforcement | Practice recurs over time | Four initial waves with at least 21 days between exposures |
| Immediate feedback | Learning follows the exact simulated click | Explain the missed cues in five minutes, without blame |
| Role relevance | Epic, ERP and IAM involve different decisions | Segment content and measure behavior within comparable groups |

## Measure behavior rather than recall

A completion certificate says someone finished content. It does not establish that they will verify a caller or report a suspicious message. Track unique clicking and reporting, repeat clicking across consecutive waves, reporting delay, timely training completion and legitimate-message reporting workload. A person may click and then report; record both and reinforce reporting after a click. A lower BEC click rate can still conceal a dangerous willingness to authorize payments.

## Safe response sequence

Pause an unexpected request involving credentials, money, patient information or remote access. Inspect the sender, but do not treat a familiar display name, copied sign-in page or successful email authentication as proof. Use a known bookmark, directory number or approved vendor contact. Report in Outlook; report promptly even if you already clicked. Do not forward the lure to coworkers.

## Ethical boundaries to remember

No credential collection, real transfers, public naming or performance-review use. Exclude patient-care crises, staff on leave, the first employment week and approved support cases. Two consecutive clicked waves alone do not authorize manager disclosure: incomplete microtraining and HR confirmation for coaching are also required. Distress calls for support and possible pause, not more pressure.

## Self-check prompts

Explain why an Epic lure can outperform generic password-expiry mail; why report speed matters alongside clicks; why “SPF passed” does not establish business legitimacy; why BEC risk cannot be summarized by click rate; and why punishment can undermine early reporting.
