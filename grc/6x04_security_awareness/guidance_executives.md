# Security Quick Reference: Executives

[MedDefense branding placeholder]  
Version 1.0 · 2026-11-10 · Draft for approval

## Your Biggest Threats

- Executive impersonation and wire fraud: a CFO-style request rushes a payment or banking change. Watch for pressure to bypass independent approval.
- Spear phishing: a confidential board or legal document arrives unexpectedly. Watch for unusual sender/reply details and urgency framed as secrecy.
- Travel exposure: a compromised account or visible screen can expose board information and authorize operational damage. Watch for unsolicited travel sign-ins and public viewing.

## What to Do Right Now

- Require a known-number callback and normal finance approval for every unusual money, credential or confidential-document request; instruct assistants to do the same.
- Use the established board portal for sensitive material and verify unexpected document requests through the office directory.
- Before travel, confirm managed-device access with IT; use the approved VPN, keep the device with you and shield the screen in public.

## When Something Looks Wrong

Report it to Security: select the suspicious email in Outlook and use the phishing report button; for a call, access concern or unavailable button, call **ext. 4-SECURITY**. Report even after clicking. You do not need to prove an attack.

## What Not to Do

- Do not approve wire transfers or banking changes solely by email or phone.
- Do not treat a reply in the same thread or a familiar display name as verification.
- Do not move board documents into personal email/cloud or approve unexpected sign-in prompts while travelling.

## Contact

Security: **ext. 4-SECURITY · security@meddefense.org**. Outlook: select the message, then choose the organization's phishing report button. Exact label and ribbon/menu location must be confirmed by IT and inserted before publication; the supplied material does not specify them.
