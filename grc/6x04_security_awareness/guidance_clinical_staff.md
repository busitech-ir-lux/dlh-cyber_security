# Security Quick Reference: Clinical Staff

[MedDefense branding placeholder]  
Version 1.0 · 2026-11-10 · Draft for approval

## Your Biggest Threats

- Epic access threats: a message says chart access will stop before your shift. Watch for an unexpected verification link, even when it names patient records.
- Fake vendor support: someone calls during an equipment or Epic problem and asks for screen access. Watch for a request without a verified support ticket.
- Workstation exposure: an unfamiliar USB appears near a nurses’ station or someone can read your screen. Watch for loose media and visible patient details.

## What to Do Right Now

- Open Epic through your normal application route; confirm unexpected access warnings using the known helpdesk directory entry.
- Before any vendor screen access, call back using the approved vendor list and confirm the ticket with IT.
- Lock the workstation before stepping away, shield patient information from nearby viewers and give found USB devices to IT without connecting them.

## When Something Looks Wrong

Report it to Security: select the suspicious email in Outlook and use the phishing report button; for a call, access concern or unavailable button, call **ext. 4-SECURITY**. Report even after clicking. You do not need to prove an attack.

## What Not to Do

- Do not enter Epic credentials from an email link.
- Do not connect found USB devices to clinical workstations.
- Do not let an unverified caller view a screen or make you skip patient-care priorities.

## Contact

Security: **ext. 4-SECURITY · security@meddefense.org**. Outlook: select the message, then choose the organization's phishing report button. Exact label and ribbon/menu location must be confirmed by IT and inserted before publication; the supplied material does not specify them.
