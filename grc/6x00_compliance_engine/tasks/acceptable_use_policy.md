## Purpose

This policy protects MedDefense patient information, systems, and clinical services.
It defines the rules each authorized user must follow so access remains accountable
and patient care remains safe.

## Scope and acceptable use

This policy applies to employees, contractors, vendors with remote access, and
temporary clinical staff who use any MedDefense account, endpoint, network, or
hosted service. MedDefense systems and accounts are for MedDefense business and
authorized clinical care only. Users must work within their assigned role, use
approved applications, and lock unattended screens.

## Prohibited activities

Users must not:

- Access a patient's record outside an active care relationship, unless a specific assigned billing,
  records, compliance, or other lawful duty authorizes that access. Curiosity, friendship, and
  family relationships do not authorize access.
- Share credentials, approve another person's sign-in request, or let anyone use their account.
- Install software on a MedDefense endpoint without IT Security authorization.
- Connect a personal device to the clinical network without written IT Security authorization.
- Send or store MedDefense data through personal email, consumer cloud storage, or unapproved
  messaging applications.
- Attempt to reach systems, data, or network segments beyond their assigned role.
- Enter or process patient data in an AI tool, external web service, or third-party platform without
  an executed BAA approved by Legal and written IT Security approval for that use.
- Disable endpoint protection, logging, encryption, or other required safeguards without an approved
  maintenance authorization.

The limited assigned-duty exception for patient record access permits authorized
work such as billing; it does not permit browsing unrelated records. Emergency
access must follow the approved emergency procedure and be logged for review.

## PHI handling obligations

Use only the patient information needed for your assigned task and share it only
with authorized recipients through approved systems, following MedDefense's
minimum necessary access rules. Verify the recipient and patient before sending
information, keep screens and printouts out of public view, and follow the Data
Classification Policy for storage and disposal.

These are MedDefense handling rules. HIPAA's minimum necessary standard has legal
exceptions, including disclosures to or requests by providers for treatment; it
does not authorize staff to browse records without a work purpose. See
[HHS minimum necessary guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/minimum-necessary-requirement/index.html).

## Remote access

Remote access requires a manager-approved work purpose, IT Security authorization,
an individual account, multifactor authentication, an approved managed device,
and the MedDefense VPN. Devices must have current security updates, disk encryption,
and active endpoint protection. Users must not access MedDefense systems from
public or shared Wi-Fi unless the VPN connection is active; they must stop work if
that connection fails. Users must prevent others from viewing patient information
and must not download it to personal devices.

## Immediate reporting

Report suspicious email, unusual system behavior, a lost or stolen device, or an
accidental PHI disclosure immediately upon discovery. Open an IT Security ticket
through the approved internal service desk and phone IT Security through the
internal directory's security incident contact. If ticket access is unavailable,
phone first and ask the responder to record the incident; do not delay reporting
to collect proof or attempt your own investigation.

Include the discovery time, affected system, and a safe callback contact. Do not
put patient details in the ticket subject or forward suspicious attachments unless
the responder directs you. The materials do not supply a ticket URL or phone
number; IT Security must publish and test both contact routes before distribution.

## Enforcement and accountability

IT Security records violations, preserves relevant evidence, and may suspend access
to contain immediate risk. HR applies the disciplinary process consistently, up to
and including termination; contractor and vendor violations may lead to removal
of access or contract action. Violations may also constitute HIPAA violations and,
depending on the conduct and applicable law, result in personal liability.

## Acknowledgment and review

Employees must sign at onboarding before system access is granted and annually
thereafter. Contractors, remote vendors, and temporary clinical staff must accept
these rules before access and on annual renewal. IT Security and HR retain dated
acknowledgments and track nonresponse; access must not be granted or renewed until
acknowledgment is recorded. A signature does not replace required security training.

IT Security reviews this policy annually and after a relevant incident. The CISO
approves revisions, and managers communicate them to affected users. IT Security
retains approved versions, distribution records, and acknowledgments for at least
six years from creation or last effective date, whichever is later.
